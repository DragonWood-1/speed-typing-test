    How Typing Speed Is Measured

    By long-standing convention, a "word" in a typing test is defined as five characters, including spaces. This standard means that a passage full of short words and a passage full of long words can be compared fairly. Your words per minute is calculated by taking the number of characters you typed, dividing by five to get standardized words, and then dividing by the number of minutes elapsed.

    • Gross WPM counts every character you typed, correct or not. It reflects your raw finger speed.
    • Net WPM subtracts your uncorrected errors, giving a truer picture of useful output.
    • Accuracy is the percentage of words you typed correctly out of all the words you attempted.

    If you want to calculate these numbers yourself from your own practice sessions, our WPM calculator and accuracy calculator walk through the exact formulas step by step.

    What Is a Good Typing Speed?

    The average person types somewhere between 35 and 45 words per minute. Reaching 60 to 70 WPM puts you comfortably above average and is more than fast enough for most professional work. Skilled typists reach 80 to 100 WPM, and the fastest typists in the world exceed 120 WPM while maintaining high accuracy. Wherever you are starting from, the key is consistent practice — even a few minutes a day produces steady, measurable improvement over a few weeks.

    How to Improve Your Typing Speed

    Getting faster is less about raw effort and more about building the right habits. These are the techniques that make the biggest difference:

    • Use the correct finger positions. Rest your fingers on the home row (ASDF for the left hand, JKL; for the right) and let each finger cover its assigned keys.
    • Do not look at the keyboard. Touch typing — typing without looking at your hands — is the single most important skill for speed. It feels slow at first but pays off enormously.
    • Prioritize accuracy over speed. Slow down enough to type correctly. Speed follows naturally once accurate movements become automatic.
    • Practice a little every day. Short, regular sessions build muscle memory far better than occasional long ones.
    • Review your mistakes. Our misspelled-word report shows exactly which words trip you up, so you can focus your practice where it counts.

    How is my typing speed calculated?

    We count the characters you typed, divide by five to get standardized words, and divide by the time elapsed in minutes. Net WPM additionally subtracts your errors, so your score reflects accurate typing rather than raw speed alone.
